## What this settles

1. **The gate is not a training-budget problem.** Accuracy, F1 and geometry all improve to about 1,000 steps
   and then degrade: the exactness count stays at zero nonempty boards at every checkpoint measured
   (400, 1,000, 12,600, 12,800), including the thresholds swept over the full grid.
2. **The best measured point is 1,000 steps**, not 20,000. Development F1 0.8044 at best threshold with
   1.17-cell geometry is the strongest result this recipe has produced; by 12,600 the same weights give
   F1 0.6373 with 4.52-cell geometry and the training loss has climbed from ~7 to ~15.
3. **The chain was stopped at 12,800/20,000 on that measurement**, not on a resource or plumbing failure.
   Spending the remaining hours would have produced a worse model than the one already on disk.

## The 220-step illusion

Earlier segments appeared to hang near step 220. They were not hanging: each segment rebuilt the
512-frame pool first, so the wall clock was dominated by pool construction and the visible step counter
had barely moved. `--pool-cache` now persists the pool once, and a 90 s faulthandler watchdog converts
any genuine silence into a full stack dump instead of an unexplained stop.

## Defect found and fixed in the chain

Segment 17 aborted with `ValueError: finite board-conditioned component query required`. The engine guard
was right: one batch produced non-finite board values inside the forward pass. No backward ran, so every
saved weight tensor is finite (checked: 269 tensors, zero non-finite, all 20 saved states). The trainer now
counts and skips such a batch at forward, loss and gradient-normalisation, saves rolling state across the
skip, and reports `skipped_batches` in each result.

## Next lever this points to

Since fit is not the binding constraint, the same gate should be run on a *fresh* pool (different samples,
not more epochs of the same 512) to separate memorisation from capability, and the occupancy/length
balance term is the part that degrades first as steps grow.
